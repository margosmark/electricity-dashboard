# Bill pace — slice 7 evidence notes

**Question Mark asked:** "I don't know what to make of this — am I on pace?"

Two things were wrong before this slice, and both were things a screenshot
could show you but no test could catch:

1. **The chart ended at the last reading.** The x-axis was a picture of the
   past — you could not see where the line was going, so the card could not
   answer its own title. Its explainer even said *"projected to Oct 16"* while
   the yellow line stopped at today.
2. **The money table's `YOU PAID` header sat above an unbilled row.** The
   row's cell said `$0.00`, so the table asserted, in a column headed "You
   paid", that you had paid nothing for days the utility has not billed yet.

## What changed

**The chart has a future.** The axis now runs to the bill date — reached by
median billed-duration stepped forward from the last bill (`file-cadence`),
or the user's billing day when there are no bills (`billing-day`), never
invented when there is neither (`period-end` + a note saying so). Over that
runway: a yellow dashed pace line to the projected landing, painted over a
black casing 3px wider (yellow-on-white is 1.4:1 — the contrast is a
test, not a taste call), and a dotted 3-bill benchmark with its value
labelled on the line.

**The headline leads.** `$271.07 by Oct 16` in the first line, with a
pacing chip that states direction *and* figure against the benchmark
(`PACING BELOW BY $65.02`) — green below, red above, grey with no bills to
compare against, in which case the chip says exactly that rather than
comparing to nothing. The explainer below it names all three lines, and
says what the projection is made from.

**The table cannot be misread.** The unbilled row labels itself
`Est. commodity ~$149.56` in the cell that used to say `$0.00`; a new
`EST. TOTAL` column prices the same row all-in from the bill's own
auto-derived rates (`~$251.07`) with a permanent `ESTIMATE` badge on the
header; and `COVERED %` → `DATA COVERAGE`, because it always meant
interval-data coverage, never "of the bill".

**The zero-delivery case stays honest.** `deriveExtraCharges` returns null
when the file states no delivery lines and there are no stored rates. Then
there is no estimate to give, and the cell shows `—` and nothing else.
`Est. total 0` would be a lie dressed as a number.

## Fixtures — why a new one was needed

Wave R's own notes admitted the gap this slice closes: *"neither committed
fixture has an unbilled tail, so the curve's non-empty state has never run
against real XML."* All four fixtures ended their readings on or before the
last bill. So `usage-summary-open-tail.xml` = the three audited Wave L bills,
verbatim, plus 2,856 hourly readings running a month past the last one —
the mid-period shape of a real export. Generated by a committed script
(`webapp/scripts/generate-open-tail-fixture.mjs`) from a fixed load shape so
the file is byte-stable, provenance recorded in the fixtures README, and
`OpenPeriod.test.tsx` pins the tail it exists to provide — a fixture that
quietly loses its tail would put every browser check back into the empty
state without failing anything else.

## Verification

`evidence/bill-pace-verify.cjs` — **47/47 checks** against a production
`next start` build, desktop 1440 and phone 390:

- card asks the question; headline is a real `$X by <date>` with a real number;
- explainer names all three lines; chip's direction, tone, and dollar figure
  agree with each other;
- three lines draw; pace is `#FACC15` and dashed; geometry is finite and
  monotone; **the pace line's `x2` exceeds the accrual's — the chart really
  does reach past today**; benchmark draws across the full width;
- OPEN cell says `Est. commodity` and never `You paid`; `EST. TOTAL` badged;
  coverage header named; est-total > commodity when rates exist;
- 0px horizontal overflow at both widths; sparkline geometry finite; toggle
  is a real 44px touch target, `aria-expanded` flips both ways, tap reveals
  the full chart and hides the sparkline.

Two things worth recording because they cost time and will cost it again:

- **A horizontal `<line>` has bbox height 0** (`y1 === y2`), so Playwright's
  `isVisible()` reports the dotted benchmark invisible while it renders
  perfectly across the chart. The check asks `getClientRects().length > 0`
  instead. This is a Playwright heuristic, not a product bug.
- **Port 3100 belongs to a sibling task's `next start`.** Ours bound nothing
  and the browser was served *their* build, which 500'd on our chunk names.
  Run on 3107.
